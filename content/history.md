# Pre-Colonization

Natives from what is today called South America were exploring the southern islands of the island chain centuries before the arrival of Europeans. The Kalinagos inhabited Camahogne when Columbus sited and named the island La Conception in 1498 on his third visit to the new world. The Spanish never settled Grenada but used it as a refueling stop on their way to and from Trinidad and South America.

![Map of the Caribbean](/images/caribbean-map.jpg)

*The Caribbean Sea, The Caribbean Islands, Central and South America*

Due to the oral tradition of the Arawak speaking natives, there is little written history of the indigenous inhabitants of Grenada before their genocide. Archaeologist has discovered burial ground and artifacts such as pottery, clay figures, musical instruments, jewelry, tools, beads and petroglyph on more than 87 sites where indigenous people lived in Grenada. Some larger sites were found on the northeast coastline at Pearls and La Poterie.

Ivan Van Sertima wrote extensively in his book They came before Columbus about the Mandingo presence pre-Columbus. He wrote Mansa Abu Bakr II as Emperor of the Mandinka kingdom of Mali West Africa. He financed and was part of a marine exploration mission in search of a new world across the ocean to the west of Africa in 1310 to 1311. Van Sertima also wrote of contact between indigenous Americans and the Songhai empire of West Africa in 1462 to 1492.

The 15th-century papal bull on servitude is Dum Diversas 1452. Issued by Pope Nicholas V, it authorized Portugal's King Afonso V to conquer non-Christian lands and reduce "Saracens, pagans, and any other unbelievers" to perpetual slavery. Spain enslavement of Africans in the Caribbean began in 1506. Pope Martin's decree allowed Bartholomew De Las Casas to transport the first African to Hispaniola from Spain.

# Columbus met Africans here

Garinagos also known as the Black Caribs of Saint Vincent lived on the northeast coast of Saint Vincent. Today the Garinagos now know as the Garifuna people who live throughout Central America, maintain that they were part of a marine expedition led by Mansa Abu Bakr II Emperor of the Mandinka kingdom. 

The English author William Young wrote that the Garifuna people were Africans who were shipwrecked onboard a Spanish slave ship in the early 1600s. The Africans were rescued by the Kalinago who temporally enslaved them, upon gaining their freedom they married Kalinago and adopted their language, culture. The Black Caribs seeking their autonomy relocated to the island's densely forested northeast.

The first attempt to settle Grenada by Europeans was in June 1609, when an English expedition arrived aboard the Diana, Penelope, and Endeavour. The settlement was attacked and destroyed by the natives, who tortured and killed many of the settlers. The ships returned on 15th December 1609, forcing the few survivors to leave the island.

![Joseph Chatoyer St.Vincent](/images/joseph-chatoyer-st-vincent3.jpg)

*Grenada before Columbus: The Black Caribs*

Garinago and Kalinago resistance to the colonization of Grenada, Saint Vincent and Dominica lasted until Grenada was settled by the French in 1649. St.Vincent and Dominica were declared neutral by the French and British until the Treaty of Paris in 1763. Due to the genocidal nature of European colonization Saint Vincent and Dominica became safe havens for the Indigenous people and escaped African slaves from the other Caribbean islands.

The Black Caribs allied with the French to fight against the British in the Black Carib Wars which ended in 1797. They also traded with the French for muskets, gunpowder and balls as far north a Guadalupe. They were led by Joseph Chotoyer who died in a battle the same year 1797. The lost of their leader and starvation due to the burning of their houses and crops brought about their surrender. They were rounded up and exiled to Roatan an island off the east coast of Honduras by the British on the 5th April 1797. 

The Garifuna people and their culture can still be found today throughout Honduras, Guatemala and Belize. Joseph Chatoyer is celebrated on 14 March as the first national hero in Saint Vincent.

The island was settled as a French proprietary colony in 1649 and named La Grenade. By 1655 the indigenous population was almost completely wiped out due to wars and European diseases. # French Rule

The Code Noir from 1685 is the code France used to govern how enslaved Africans were seen and treated in the French colonies. La Grenade was mainly locally governed this afforded the Free Coloured (**French mixed race**) certain privileges. The mixed race were allowed to marry, practice Catholicism, join the militia and own land. The Code Noir stipulations mandated Africans to be baptized into Roman Catholicism which then allowed interracial marriages of white French men to free Africans women with the outcome of biracial children. The biracial children were then allowed to marry each other to create the Mulatto or young mule and the creole language.

![Private AfroCentric Tours Grenada French colony](/images/french-creole.jpg)

*Plantation life in the Francophone Caribbean*

The enslaved Africans skillfully negotiated a few privileges from the French. These privileges were used as soft power to appease the Africans and guarantee a peaceful coexistence for the French.

Africans maintained the essence of their native beliefs by adapting their spiritual practices and beliefs to a Christian frame of reference. The enslaved African used for example the Roman Catholic All Saints and All Souls to mask the reverence to their ancestors because African spirituality was disallowed by the French enslavers. The Africans lit fires and brought food and drink to the burial sites of their ancestors on these days. The practice is carried out today on All Saints and All Souls with a party atmosphere and the lighting of candles at the island's larger and family cemeteries.

In 1671 Grenada was home to 283 free colonists and 222 slaves, by the end of the Seven Years’ War in 1763, Grenada counted 646 free families and approximately 13,000 slaves. France invaded Grenada and ruled the island for the second time from 1779 to 1783. This brings to mind 200 years later 1979 to 1983.

# British Colonization

The Seven Years’ War and signing of the 1763 Treaty of Paris. France ceded a number of territories, including Quebec and Grenada, to Britain. The British Crown made Grenada the seat of a newly-constituted administrative and economic entity known as the ‘Southern Caribbean Islands. A single Governor-General was appointed to govern Grenada as well as the neighboring islands of Tobago, Dominica, and St. Vincent. St.Vincent was considered a neutral territory because the French was denied a foothold by the Black Caribs. It was only after the Black Carib war that the British fully occupied the island.

Grenada became the new name of La Grenade and now a British colony, since 1763. The island was a rich bounty for the British Crown as it enjoyed a some development under the French. The British saw the opportunity to develop the island further using sugar cane as the cash crop as sugar cane was already grown of 87 plantations locally. The labour force of 13000 slave could be increased through their involvement in the lucrative trans Atlantic slave trade.In the case of Grenada the Barbados Slave code of 1661 was the blueprint for British slavery on the island and throughout the British colony.

# Dismantling a System

Atlantic-geopolitics and the Sociol-economics in the late 18th century consisted of several revolutions, for example, the French and American revolutions. The Anti Slavery movement in Britain gained influence with prominent activists like Thomas Clarkson (1760 -1846), Olaudah Equiano (1745 - 1797), Granville Sharpe (1735-1813), William Wilberforce (1759-1833) and others. The Haitian revolution from (1791-1804) was also in progress, led by Toussaint Louverteur (1743-1833), Dutty Boukman (1767-1791), Jean-Jacques Dessalines (1758-1806),  Empress Marie-Claire Heureuse Félicité Bonheur (1758-1858), and others like Henri Chritophe a Grenadian born African (1767-1820).

The late 18th century saw numerous rebellions of enslaved Africans in the European colonies. The French revolution influenced Julien Fedon, owner of the Belvidair plantation and enslaver, the son of Pierre Fedon, a white French man and Brigitte, a free African woman. The suppression of the French free coloured by denying them the right to vote, own land and join the military, thus reducing them to second-class citizens and not being against Slavery, caused Julien Fedon to lead a rebellion against the British in 1795. The enslaved African population seeking their freedom saw this as an opportunity to free themselves.

![Grenada Slave Rebellion](/images/fedon-rebellion.jpg)

*One of many Grenadian slave Revolts*

Over 14 thousand enslaved Africans took part in the rebellion. The enslaved Africans who fought with Fedon gained revenge on their owners by burning their plantations. Free coloured and a small number of enslaved African also fought on the side of the British. Louis La Grenade (1733-1808) a free coloured, owner of Mount Jaloux estate in the parish of St.George collaborated with the British against the rebellion. Grenada was under the control of Fedons army except the parish of St,George which was the British administrative capital. Fedon rebellion ended on 17th June 1796 with the lost of 7000 lives the majority being enslaved Africans.

The British discouraged future insurrection and made an example of the white and free-coloured French Catholics, who they attempted to assimilate with special privileges. The Act of Attainder named over 460 persons, of which over 100 were sentenced to death, publicly executed in the market square in St.Georges. They were beheaded, and their heads displayed publicly. The remainder were deported and exiled for life from the British Empire. The captured enslaved Africans who took part in the rebellion were executed without trial. Julien Fedon was never captured, he was assumed drowned attempting to escape to Trinidad.

At the end of the 18th century saw the French revolution from 1789 to 1794. The Haitian revolution was from 1891 to 1804. The Fedon rebellion was from 1795 to 1796. This resistance highlighted the plight of enslaved Africans in the European colonies. The increasing slave rebellions in the Americas and the growth of the abolition movement began to influence public opinion against the enslavement of Africans. This situation forced the Europeans to rethink their labour policies due to the reliability and sustainability of slave labour. They developed a new business model that became the Industrial revolution.

The Act for the Abolition of the slave trade on 15th March 1807 abolished the slave trade. A Royal navy set up a squadron on the west coast of Africa. The slave trade felony act of 1811 made the slave trade a felony throughout the British Empire. Any slaver caught paid one hundred pounds per slave and lost the ship. The captured Africans returned to Africa and were set free. Between 1808 and 1860. the British captured over 1600 slave ships and freed over 150000 captured Africans.

Samuel Sharpe, a Baptist minister, led a rebellion in Jamaica on December 1831 that lasted ten days. The loss of lives and property damage caused an inquiry in the British parliament. The Abolition movement used the Jamaican revolt with other factors to bring about the Slavery Abolition Act of 1833, which came into law on 1st August 1834. The Act stated that slaves under six years were free, but those over six became apprentices. The first apprenticeship ended on 1st August 1838, and slavery ended in the British Colonies on 1st August 1840.

The former slaves were now free but had no means of support because they did not receive any compensation, the only employment being from their former owners. The British parliament enacted the slave compensation act of 1837, which compensated the slave owners 20 million pounds. The British taxpayer finished paying the loan in 2015. A decline in production caused by the labour shortage forced the British to seek a new labour force for their plantations.

# Indentured Labour 

Human resources now a commodity in the economic system caused the British to seek their labour throughout their colonies. Indentureship became the solution as a fixed labour cost and duration could be established with Indentureship contracts. Britain offered Indentureship contracts and secured an international pool of labour from countries like Australia, India, Africa, China, and the middle east countries like Lebanon and Syria. 

![Indentured Indian Labour](/images/indian-indentureship.jpg)

*Indentured Indian Plantation Labourers*

Grenada saw an influx of indenture labour not only to solve any labour problem but also to establish a base cost for labour and by offering land at the end of certain contracts future labour was secured as the workers became residents and employees. The plantation system continued for the ex-slaves as the now redundant African only option was to seek employment on the plantations they left as slaves for a minimum wage.

Africans now arrived as indenture labour and as freed Africans from the underground trade in African after Emancipation. Indentureship allowed group migration which was not practice during slavery to avoid communication between Africans of the same ethnic group. This period saw African from different groups contracting themselves and migrating to Grenada bring the African culture that the Trans Atlantic stave trade erased. The Yoruba, Akan, Ashanti, workers brought their culture and traditions to add to Grenada's inter racial oil-down.

Christians from Syria, Lebanon and other middle and far east countries like Australia a British penal colony, China after the British China war were also recruited through Indentureship to serve in different sectors of the plantation industry.

On the 1st May 1857, the first 287 Indian indentured workers arrived at Owens Bay in St.Patrick. By 1885 over 3033 indentured Indian workers were brought to Grenada from the northern region of India. 

Indentureship contracts lasted for three years and could extend to 5 years meant a 9-hour day from Monday to Friday and a half day on Saturdays, which included housing and health care. If extended to 5 years, in some cases they received a small plot of land at the end of the contract they would receive a free return passage to their country of origin. Around 80% of the Indian indentured workers decided to stay in Grenada.

# The Scramble for Africa

The year 1885 saw the conclusion of the Berlin conference, where the leaders of Europe agreed on colonizing the African continent and how they would enslave the Africans in Africa. The European world is now in the process of transforming from a mercantile system to a capitalistic system. In the capitalist system, labour is a resource like any other material. Africa became the source of the materials and labour for the extraction needed to develop the industrial revolution, the new business model for Europe's development.

Grenada, a British colony, became redundant and dependent on the British crown. The raw materials that the island produced are now obtainable at a lower price on the world market due to the economy of scale. The plantation owners found new investments and migrated their capital away from Grenada. The plantations were left to managers and overseers as absentee landlords looked for investment opportunities elsewhere.

![Harvesting Cocoa](/images/cocoa-harvest-grenada.jpg)

*The Cocoa Harvest*

The labour force of redundant free African slaves became employable as cheap labour. England, Scotland and Ireland were the source for poor whites to work alongside and oversee the ex-slaves and indenture plantation workers. Grenada cash crops became sugar cane, cocoa and spices like nutmeg, cloves, cinnamon, pimento and ginger. The island's topography, climatic conditions and proximity to England made Grenada ideal for cultivating these spices.

Grenada's history from 1885 to 1979 is marked by significant social, economic, political, and geopolitical changes that shaped the island nation's trajectory. This period saw Grenada transition from a British colony to an independent nation, amidst global and regional shifts in power and ideology.

# Social Developments

In 1885, Grenada's capital, St. George's, became the capital of the British Windward Islands, centralizing colonial administration and fostering a sense of Grenadian identity. The abolition of slavery in 1833 led to a gradual social restructuring, as former slaves sought to establish their own communities and livelihoods. However, the legacy of slavery persisted, with many Grenadians working in low-paying agricultural jobs, often on the same plantations where their ancestors were enslaved.

---

# Economic Changes

The economy of Grenada in this period was heavily reliant on agriculture, particularly the cultivation of spices such as nutmeg and cocoa. The early 20th century saw fluctuations in commodity prices, which impacted the island's economic stability. Efforts to diversify the economy were slow, and the reliance on a single crop made Grenada vulnerable to market changes and natural disasters.

---

# Political Movements

Politically, the early 20th century was a time of growing awareness and activism among the Grenadian population. The formation of the Grenada United Labour Party in 1950 by Eric Gairy marked the beginning of a more organized political landscape. Gairy's leadership, however, became increasingly authoritarian, leading to widespread discontent and calls for change.

---

# Geopolitical Context
Grenada's geopolitical significance grew during the Cold War, as the Caribbean became a focal point for ideological battles between the East and West. The rise of the New Jewel Movement in 1979, which led to the overthrow of Gairy's government, was a direct response to the political oppression and economic stagnation that had characterized much of the island's recent history.

The culmination of these social, economic, and political factors was Grenada's independence from Britain in 1974,

In conclusion, the period from 1885 to 1979 in Grenada was one of profound transformation. The island grappled with its colonial legacy while striving for self-determination and development. The social, economic, political, and geopolitical events of this time laid the groundwork for the modern state of Grenada

# Grenada Revolution

The revolt started on the 13th of March 1979 with the takeover of the island administration, this made Grenada the only English-speaking country in the 20th century to successfully depose British rule. The New Jewel Movement became the People Revolutionary Government led by Maurice Bishop a young and charismatic lawyer. Geopolitics turned Grenada into a pariah state because the government was not democratically elected and would not re-instate the elected government.

Cuba became one of the first allies of the Maurice Bishop government which brought Grenada in direct conflict with the United States. Fidel Castro and the Cuban people extended the hand of friendship and assistance in the development of the people and the country. In a short span of time Grenada showed substantial growth not only in its economy but also in its human resources for example by improving the literacy rate, students were given scholarships to study abroad. Agriculture and Infra-structure development became the main focus of the People Revolutionary Government.

![Grenada Education 1979](/images/grenada-educationposter.jpg)

*A People Revolutionary Government Bill Board*

Institutions such as The National Insurance Scheme, Nation Cooperation Banking, The Nation Marketing and Importing Board, has their genesis from the P.R.G. Agra-processing spurred Agriculture production and a manufacturing industry. This boost the local economy and trade in the form of export mainly barter. Tourism became the industry of the future and infra-structure like an International airport was necessary but unavailable locally.

Grenada embarked on a development drive with the support of the grass root in the communities with assistance from the people of Cuba and their President Fidel Castro. Groups were formed to organize the people in the implication of various programs. Women and juvenile groups were popular and carried out numerous function in the communities. Defence became a priority and a Defence force and a militia was organized to protect the country.

Within a short period industries were established or regenerated like Agriculture where the P.R.G. began obtaining indebted estates and establishing an Argo-processing plants, Construction of the International airport, Affordable housing, The health sector was improved with Cuban assistance, qualified students were sent on foreign scholarships. The nation started seeing improvement in living standards and a glimpse of prosperity.

![Maurice Bishop International airport](/images/mbia-1983.jpg)

*Construction of the runway: Maurice Bishop International Airport*

Caribbean nations were now paying attention to Grenada development with some seeing the P.R.G. as an alternative to their form of government. The proximity to Cuba and by default Russia brought the island into the geopolitical focus of the Cold War which made it a threat to western hegemony and the Monroe Doctrine of the USA. A propaganda war began with rhetoric being the weapon of choice.

Counter revolution and destabilization brought on infighting within the P.R.G. government which culminated in the execution of Maurice Bishop along with nine members of his cabinet including a pregnant female on 19.10.1983 at Fort George in Grenada's capital. Hudson Philip as head of the P.R.A. or the People Revolutionary Army brought the country under military rule and ordered an island wide curfew.

On the 25.10.1983 the island awoke to the sound of aircraft and bombs. American troops with the help of 6 Caribbean nation invaded Grenada. Urgent Fury lasted till 2.11.1983 and the US left by mid-December 1983 leaving noncombat troops as advisors on the island.

# Neo Liberalism

The Transition period meant eliminating all that the P.R.G. period produced except the airport Construction and some Institutions formed by the Revolutionary Government. All other traces of the Revolution was destroyed even the body of the Revolutionary Prime minister Maurice Bishop has not been found to the present day. The period began with a transitional phase led by Sir Paul Scoon as Governor General before election was held.

Prime Minister Margret Thatcher of England and President Ronald Reagan of the US introduced an economically system where wealth would be transferred from the Public into Private ownership. This is the birth of Neo Liberalism which is to become the model of government to be practiced in England and the English-speaking world. Grenada as a transitioning nation became an ideal candidate for this experiment.

![Margret Thatcher and Ronald Reagan](/images/maggie-and-ronald.jpg)

*Prime Minister Margret Thatcher and President Ronald Regan*

NNP or New National Party led by Herbert Blaize won the first post P.R.G. election and formed the new government. The island regained political stability and constitutional rule and ushered in the Neo Liberal agenda attempting to rebuild the economy promoting agriculture and tourism as the main focus.

The changing political landscape and the death of Herbert Blaize in 1989 saw the N.D.C. Nation Democratic Congress winning the 1990 election and Nicolas Brathwaite becoming the Prime Minister and was succeeded by George Brizan. After the events of the 80s Grenada sought reconstruction also reconciliation seeking economic recovery and re-engagement with international institutions.

Doctor Keith Mitchell became the leader of the N.N.P. and Prime minister when N.N.P. won the 1995 election and held government until 2008. N.D.C. under Tilman Thomas leadership won the election that year, but the party imploded and saw NNP being returned to government winning every seat in the election. Dr.Mitchell would form the government as Prime minister until 2022. At present Hon.Dickon Mitchell is one of the youngest Prime ministers in the world along with a very young parliament.

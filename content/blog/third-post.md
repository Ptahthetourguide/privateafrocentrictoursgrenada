# Showcasing Jab Culture

### Jab Jab Today

Grenada Jab Jab has grown into a devoted following and a cultural revival, now recognized as a central part of Grenadian identity. What began as the embodiment of rebellion has become an exportable Grenadian product, celebrated worldwide as part of our nation’s culture. This brings to mind a question my grandmother often asked: **"What going on here?"**

---

# Origins of Grenada Jab Jab

As a student of history, I searched **Our Story** to discover the origins of Jab Jab. The name came from the Creole‑speaking French Caribbean islands. *La Jables* is remembered in Grenadian folklore as a well‑dressed woman with cow hooves for feet, luring men and boys into the forest. Victims lost their memories and could not recall what *La Jables* had done to them. 

![Grenada La Jables](/images/la-jables.jpg)

*La Jables The Jab Jab Empress*

The French introduced the concept of **La Jables** to Grenada, but Africans named her husband Jab. Slaves were denied literacy, yet Africans preserved their culture through their keepers of knowledge, the Griots and elders. This resilience survived the Middle Passage and lives on today in [Grenada Carnival](https://spicemasgrenada.com/), where Jab and his Empress La Jables dominate the scene.

# Grenada Carnival and Soca
Grenada’s youth have embraced Jab Jab culture, witnessed annually before dawn on J’ouvert morning. Thousands of Grenadians take part in this ancestral practice, now shared with the world. Every Grenadian of African descent knows instinctively what to do when the conch shell sounds and the drums beat. It is in our blood, handed down by our progenitors.

Nationally, Jab Jab fuels a large part of the island's creativity industry especially in media and music among Grenada’s younger generation. The Jab Jab rhythm drives Soca, the most popular local music genre, as the Jab Jab rhythm  powers Grenada Carnival musically. 

Grenada is now a force on the world stage for Soca. The Isle of Spice has produced world‑renowned artists in Calypso and Soca, including the Mighty Sparrow, born Slinger Francisco in Grand Roy, St. John. Tallpree was the first Grenadian artist to showcase the Jab Jab rhythm internationally, placing third in the 2000 International Soca Monarch in Trinidad and Tobago. Since then, Mr. Killa has won one of the Power Soca Monarch title on of the highest awards in Soca music.

# Jab Jab a Gifts to the World
Grenadians and their descendants have contributed to Caribbean history and culture in numerous ways. One field where Grenadians have excelled is in southern Caribbean music and entertainment. Carnival existed in the African francophone Caribbean before migrating to other parts of the world to becaome one of the largest street festivals on the planet. 

The Mighty Sparrow the Calypso King of the world was born in Grand Roy St.John Grenada. The steel pan is the only musical instrument invented in the 20th century was invented in Trinidad and Tobago. WITCO Desperadoes from Laventille Port of Spain — a community with strong Grenadian ties is the best Steel band in T&T after Renegades.

# Grenada Jab Jab Experience
In 2019, Mr. Killa triumphed in the Power Soca Monarch, reminding the Caribbean of Grenada’s cultural roots. His victory highlighted how Grenada’s Jab Jab rhythm is not just local heritage but a regional force.

Grenada’s youth actively promote Jab Jab culture on social media, opening international markets for [Grenada Carnival](https://spicemasgrenada.com/),, and opportunities for artists to showcase their talent globally. Private AfroCentric Tours offer  [Private Tours](/tours) that include J’ouvert participation, storytelling sessions, and visits to historic sites. Visitors learn that Jab Jab is more than a masquerade — it is a living tradition, a spiritual practice, and a cultural inheritance.

Cultures evolve or fade, but Jab Jab has endured since the plantations of French colonization. Its survival is a testament to Grenadians’ resilience in preserving African culture and identity.

Grenada, Carriacou, and Petit Martinique form one of the smallest nations in the Western Hemisphere — a nation blessed with the inheritance of our forebearers, who toiled and endured to create paradise. They labored under some of the worst circumstances known to man so that we could be here today.

# It Is in We Blood

![Grenada Secondary School Students](/images/school-boys.jpg)

*Students of PBC Secondary School after Carnival*

As a nation, we inherited land and a mixture of cultures, but African heritage has always been predominant in Grenada. Our ancestors were kidnapped from their homeland, endured the horrors of the Middle Passage, and survived slavery. Their resilience lives on in Grenada Jab Jab and Grenada Carnival today.

As a nation, we inherited the land; as a people, we inherited a mixture of cultures from various nations around the world. African culture has always been predominant in Grenada. The problem was that we were not allowed to show it or even think of its dominance.

It began with raids in the middle of the night, when houses were burned and sleeping villagers captured. They were marched miles to underground dungeons on the coast, branded, and kept awaiting sale, the Middle Passage, the auction block, chattel slavery, and plantation life.

Grenada has a population of more than 110,000, with around 95% of African descent. The diaspora is even larger, spreading Grenadian culture worldwide. Grenada, Carriacou, and Petit Martinique form one of the smallest nations in the Western Hemisphere, yet one blessed with the inheritance of our fore bearers who labored under unimaginable conditions to create paradise.

We call ourselves Grenadian, but with the inherited knowledge that Africa is our Motherland. **We are African because they were African.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does La Jables still exist in Grenada today?**  
A: There has not been any recent sighting in Grenada or the sister islands of the cow hoofed variety. I am sure that she still exist in the minds of Grenadians through folklore.

**Q: Is Jab Jab culture still relevant to Grenadians?**  
A: Yes especially to Grenadians because our ancestors resisted and suffered to preserve our culture and Jab Jab is a part of our identity.

**Q: How has Jab Jab influenced Soca music?**  
A: The Jab Jab rhythm drives Soca, inspiring Grenadian artists like Tallpree and Mr. Killa, who took Grenada’s Jab Jab rhythm international.

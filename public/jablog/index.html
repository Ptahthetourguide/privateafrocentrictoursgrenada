    Jab Jab After Slavery

    Emancipated but Still Not Free

    Carnival as we know it today originated from the Roman celebration of Carna Vale, which French patois transformed into Carnival. Catholic planters explained this to their government as a Christian lesson of compassion — giving their slaves leftovers and one day off work each year to indulge, celebrate, and socialize on the plantations.

    Emancipation came seventy‑three years after Grenada became a British colony in 1763 following the Treaty of Paris. This left a large number of Creolized Africans and mixed‑race French on the island. The Fedon revolt of 1795 reduced their numbers through fighting, executions, exile, and migration. The revolt left the island divided along national and religious lines. The emancipated Africans had a choice: Catholic or Anglican, French or British.

    Jab Jab: The Akan Spirit of Spicemas

    Living Archaeology

    The conch shell sounds as latecomers arrive for the pouring of old oil. Once everyone is blackened, they visit the coffin to release the intoxicating spirit — rum — and pour libation to invite the ancestors. As the first rays of sun appear, it is J’ouvert morning.

    This is the moment Jab Jab lives for: to play Mas. The warm‑up begins with another drink of rum. Now come the important parts of every Jab Jab costume — the horns, chain, and red mouth. J’ouvert starts with the sun and a pulsating Jab Jab rhythm, leading into the call‑and‑response chant. The conch shell conducts the rhythm section of old kitchen utensils and anything that can hold the beat. That is when this Spice nation starts Playing Mas.

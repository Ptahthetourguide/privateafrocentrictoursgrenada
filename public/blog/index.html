Emancipated but Still Not Free Jab Jab After Slavery Carnival as we know it today originated from the Roman celebration of Carna Vale, which French patois transformed into Carnival. Catholic planters explained this to their government as a Christian lesson of compassion — giving their slaves leftovers and one day off work each year to indulge, celebrate, and socialize on the plantations. Emancipation came seventy‑three years after Grenada became a British colony in 1763 following the Treaty of Paris.

Jab Jab: Living Archaeology The conch shell sounds as latecomers arrive for the pouring of the old oil. Once everyone is blackened, they visit the coffin to release the intoxicating spirit — rum — and pour libation to invite the ancestors. As the first rays of sun appear, it is J’ouvert morning. This is the moment Jab Jab lives for: to play Mas. The warm‑up begins with another drink of rum.

Jab Jab after Slavery

Emancipated but still not Free

Carnival as we know it today originated from the Roman celebration of Carna Vale which French patois turned into Carnival. Catholic planter could explain this to their Government as a Christian lesson of compassion. Giving their slaved their left over and one day off work a year to indulge, celebrate and socialize on their plantations.

Emancipation came seventy three years after Grenada became a British colony in seventeen sixty three following the Treaty of Paris. This left a large number of Creolized Africans and mixed race French on the island. The Fedon revolt in seventeen ninety five reduced their numbers due to fighting the British, executions, exile, and migration. The revolt left the island population divided along national and religious lines. The emancipated African had a choice Catholic or Anglican, French or British.

Evangelism and the introduction of the Slave Bible to the West Indies causing Grenadians to convert to Christianity and forget Anansi. Many chose Roman Catholic as resistance to the Protestant British. Anansi’s festival Carnival was created during French Catholic colonization and could only continue as a Religious festival. They then leveraged being Roman Catholic to make Carnival a day of celebration. Some became Anglican to have representation under the British.

Religion complicate Jab Jab existence because the culture would now be demonized to make it became the symbol of fear for Christians..

Carnival returned on Emancipation Day to celebrate the end of chattel slavery and became a celebration of freedom. Jab Jab became an individual or small groups of masquerades. Their costume became a depiction of the Biblical Devil with horn to the newly Christianized Africans.

The culture is now seen as bad, evil, and worshiping the devil with his kingdom in a pit of fire called hell. Fear caused the Africans to convert to Christianity and forget Anansi.

They called it Colonization and made themselves the rulers of the system by inventing Capitalism and making the African their capital. The ex-slaves were thrown into a Capitalist economy with no capital because they were the capital. The only difference was that they knew their strength because one has to be strong to survive chattel slavery.

Jab Jab culture followed Grenadians and Carnival to be found in numerous large capitols and cities around the world where Grenadians migrated. It was created on the ex-slave plantation island Grenada. Inherited from a dispossessed people determined to survive through all generations of which I am proud to say I am one. An African Survivor, a Grenadian Jab.

Jab Jab is the Devil

Early emancipation was a time of transition for the island and its people and change meant adjusting to the times. Religion would now play a major role in the destruction of African spirituality by capturing the African mind. Once in control of the mind there is no need for bondage because fear could be injected directly into the mind. If applied properly fear would make them run away from African spirituality because only the Christian god could save them from their oppressors.

Jab Jab saw rapid decline as the people flocked to the churches to learn about the Christian god. The Protestant church of England role in the West Indian colonies became one of conversion. The lure of worshiping the colonizers god gave the African a sense of equality with their colonizers. They became convinced that Jab Jab is the biblical devil and very bad thing awaited them if they worship the devil.

Emancipation Day eighteen thirty four was the first Carnival in Grenada. (I could imagine the Mas the ancestors played that day and the following days). In eighteen seventy seven Grenada held it first official Carnival festival held under British rule. Anansi Temple was rebuilt and Jab Jab reappeared publicly with the sound of conch shell J’ouvert morning calling Grenadians to worship and play Mas.

It is a fact that the churches profited and is still profiting from slavery. Pope Nicolas V started and Queen Elizabeth I and the Bishop of Canterbury mastered the slave trade. The post Emancipation era brought new challenges The churches play a major role with the government in shaping Laws and educating the island children. Schools were mainly faith based and ended for most at the primary level. Parents were happy to give their children an education which they as parents never had.

What parents did not realized was that indoctrination and selection in the form of grading was the agender. This was designed to shape the young minds by teaching them Religion to distract them from their spirituality by turning Anansi into fairy tale.

Jab Jab Costume is Black and Dirty

Freedom of an enslaved people can not be dressed up so the Jab has no costume because our ancestors were only afforded at best two sets of garments. The clothes and everything else including the slaves on the plantation belonged to the master. Punishment was the reward for mishandling anything that belonged to the master.

Mud or charcoal is what was used to cover themselves so it could be washed out later plus as disguise against later repercussions from Master. Today used vehicle oil is what is used for the black shining effect but molasses was used when sugar cane was the main agricultural product before motorized vehicle were invented. That Jab Jab is called the Jab malasie. As Carnival developed and became international colored Jab Jab emerged from black to bands of multi color Jab Jab. Grenada maintained black as the colour in keeping with tradition.

As dispossess people their only access was to disused items that the master gave them permission to use. Symbolism and color was used to paint the picture that carried the message of “This is the Devil” through the generations.

Early masqueraders made their helmets from disused chamber pots with attached cow horns. The message that Do not put shit in your head Red dye to colour and drip from their mouths. I heard blood suckers. The conch shell to me is the signal to wakeup. The drums are the heart beat of the ancestors which we carry in the coffin with the other spirits especially the liquid intoxicating spirit. The chains that we must break everyday to be free.

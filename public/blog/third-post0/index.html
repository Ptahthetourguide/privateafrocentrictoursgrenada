Grenada Jab Jab Culture Today

Grenada Jab Jab has grown into a devoted following and a cultural revival, now recognized as a central part of Grenadian identity. What began as the embodiment of rebellion has become an exportable Grenadian product, celebrated worldwide as part of our nation’s culture. This brings to mind a question my grandmother often asked: “What’s going on here?”

Origins of Grenada Jab Jab

As a student of history, I searched for Our Story and discovered the origins of Jab Jab. The name came from the Creole-speaking French Caribbean islands. La Jables is remembered in Grenadian folklore as a well-dressed woman with cow hooves for feet, luring men and boys into the forest. Victims lost their memories and could not recall what La Jables had done to them.

The French introduced La Jables to Grenada, but Africans named her counterpart Jab. Slaves were denied literacy, yet Africans preserved their culture through Griots and elders. This resilience carried across the Middle Passage and lives on today in Grenada Carnival, where Jab Jab and his Empress dominate the scene.

Grenada Carnival and Soca Music

Grenada’s youth have embraced Jab Jab culture, witnessed annually before dawn on J’ouvert morning. Thousands of Grenadians take part in this ancestral practice, now shared with the world. Every Grenadian of African descent knows instinctively what to do when the conch shell sounds and the drums beat. It is in our blood, handed down by our progenitors.

Nationally, Jab Jab fuels creativity among Grenada’s younger generation in media and music. The Jab Jab rhythm drives Soca, the most popular local genre, and powers Grenada Carnival performances.

Grenada is now a force on the world stage for Soca. The Isle of Spice has produced world-renowned artists in Calypso and Soca, including the Mighty Sparrow, born Slinger Francisco in Grand Roy, St. John. Tallpree was the first Grenadian artist to showcase the Jab Jab rhythm internationally, placing third in the 2000 International Soca Monarch in Trinidad and Tobago. Since then, Mr. Killa has won one of the highest awards in Soca music.

Grenada Tours and Cultural Experiences

In 2019, Mr. Killa triumphed in the Power Soca Monarch, reminding the Caribbean of Grenada’s cultural roots. Trinidadians invented the steel pan, but Grenadians have contributed significantly to its legacy, with bands like Desperadoes from Laventille — a community with strong Grenadian ties.

Grenada’s youth actively promote Jab Jab culture on social media, opening international markets for Grenada Carnival, Grenada tours, and opportunities for artists to showcase their work globally.

Cultures evolve or fade, but Jab Jab has endured since the plantations of French colonization. Its survival is a testament to Grenadians’ resilience in preserving African culture and identity.

It Is in We Blood

As a nation, we inherited land and a mixture of cultures, but African heritage has always been predominant in Grenada. Our ancestors were kidnapped from their homeland, endured the horrors of the Middle Passage, and survived slavery. Their resilience lives on in Grenada Jab Jab and Grenada Carnival today.

Grenada has a population of more than 110,000, with around 95% of African descent. The diaspora is even larger, spreading Grenadian culture worldwide. Grenada, Carriacou, and Petit Martinique form one of the smallest nations in the Western Hemisphere, yet one blessed with the inheritance of our fore bearers who labored under unimaginable conditions to create paradise.

We call ourselves Grenadian, but we know Africa is our Motherland. We are African because they were African.

Jab Jab: To the World

Jab Jab Culture

Now we are seeing a devoted following and a revival of Jab Jab as it is now recognised as a part of Grenadian culture. Something that started out as the embodiment of the opposite of good, pure, and God is today an exportable Grenadian product as part of our nation’s culture. This brings to mind a question my grand mother asked me on certain occasions. “What going on here?”

As a student of history I started searching for Our Story and this is what I found and my interpretation of what I discovered about Jab Jab. The name originated in the Creole speaking French Caribbean islands. La Jables known locally from stories passed down through generations about a well dress woman with cow hoofs for feet who lured men and boy into the forest to drug them. The victims lost their memories so the could not recall what the La Jables done to them.

The word La Jables was introduced to Grenada by the French but they never told the Africans the name of her husband. The Africans named him Jab as the masculine of La Jables. Slaves did not learn European languages in schools during slavery because of forced illiteracy punishable not only to the slave but also anyone who was caught teaching a slave to read and write.

So Africans preserve their culture through keepers of knowledge, the Griots and the elders of the group. That is what was transported across the Atlantic during the Middle Passage. They used what they had and knew to preserve African identity for their future generations. To today as Jab Jab the culture known internationally from the time the Jab and his Empress appear on the Carnival scene.

Grenada’s youth has embraced the Jab Jab culture of their fore parents knowingly or otherwise. It is witnessed annually before dawn J’ouvet morning thousand of Grenadians taking part in a practice that we inherited from our ancestors and shared with the world. Every Grenadian of African decent know what to do when the conch shell sound and they hears the beat of the drum. This was not taught to us it is in our blood handed down to us by our pre-genitures.

Nationally Jab Jab is fueling a wave of creativity among the younger generation of Grenadians in the media. This is driven by the Jab Jab rhythm as background to the most popular local music genre known as Soca.

Grenada is now a factor to be reckoned with on the world stage for the Soca that is produced locally. The Isle of Spice has always produced world renowned recording artists in the southern Caribbean such a Calypso and Soca. The Calypso monarch of the world is the Mighty Sparrow born Slinger Francisco in the rural village of Grand Roy in St.John.

Tallpre was the first Grenadian artist to light up the world stage with the Jab Jab rhythm placing third in the 2000 Internation Soca Monarch competition in the Mecca for Calypso and Soca Trinidad and Tobago. Since that historic achievement Mr.Killa won the one of the highest awards in Soca music.

Mr.Killa took to the stage and demolished the competition in the 2019 Power Soca Monarch to reminded our neighbours where the Mighty Sparrow and one of the largest Carnivals in the world originated. Trinidadians also invented the only music instrument in the 20th century namely the Steel pan. Desperadoes is one of the most accomplished steel band from Laventille, a predominant Grenadian neighbourhood in Port of Spain Trinidad.

Our youth are very active in the social media spaces promoting the culture to regionally and internationally audiences. This promotion has opened up international markets for Grenada’s Carnival, products and tour opportunities for Artists.

Cultures develop or die over a period that is the nature of things. Jab Jab began on the slave plantation during French colonization and survived Grenada’s tumultuous history. This is a testament of the resilience and survival capacity of Grenadians to hold on to their African Culture and Identity.

It is in We Blood

As a nation the people inherited the land as a people we inherited a mixture of cultures from various nations around the world. African culture was always predominant in Grenada. The problem was that we were not allowed to show it or even show the thought of dominance.

Our ancestors journey started as kidnap victims in their peaceful and abundant homeland. From that moment they underwent the dispossession and erasure of their humanity to make them what they wanted them to be. To this end they applied methods worse than the Spanish Inquisition because infidels are not worthy of humanity.

It started with a march in the middle of the night because that is when the raider came to burn the houses down and exposed the sleeping villagers for capture. They walked their captives miles to reach the underground dungeons on the coast where they were branded and kept awaiting sale, the middle passage, the auction block, chattel slavery and plantation life.

We know that we are our ancestors that survived the worse crime committed against humanity. Today emerging as a strong, proud and resilient people. Our ancestors are proud that their blood, sweat and tears were not in vain. Grenada has a population of more than 110,000 with around 95% of its people being African by decent. The population living on the 3 main islands with an even larger Diaspora than the local population.

Grenada, Carriacou and Petit Martinique is one of the smallest nations in the western Hemisphere. A nation blessed with the inheritance of our forbearers that toiled and endured to create Paradise. They laboured very hard under some of the worse circumstances and conditions know to man for us to be here today. We call ourselves Grenadian but with the inherited knowledge that Africa is out Mother’s Land. We are African because they were African.

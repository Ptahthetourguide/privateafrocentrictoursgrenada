Jab Jab: The Akan Spirit of Spicemas

Living Archaeology

The conch shell sound and the late comers arrive for the pouring of the old oil. Once everyone is blacked out, they visit coffin to release the intoxicating liquid spirit the rum and pour libation to invite the ancestors to take their place. As the first rays of sun ray appears it is J’ouvet morning.

This is the moment Jab Jab live for because they will play a Mas. The warmup begins with another drink of rum. Now the important parts of every Jab Jab costume the horns, chain and red mouth. J’ouvet starts with the the sun and a pulsating Jab Jab rhythm to begin the call and repeat chant. The conch shell conducts the rest of the rhythm section of old kitchen utensils and anything that could hold the rhythm. That is when this Spice nation starts Playing Mas.

What is Jab Jab

Our ancestors developed ways to surrender their bodies but retaining their humanity. A very difficult endeavor as Chattel but the Africans lived in multiple dimensions. Jab Jab is a cultural expression that Grenadians developed and use as an antidote to the after effect of Slavery and Colonization.

The Culture developed on the slave plantations of the Creole speaking French Caribbean islands. The enslaved Africans searching for a solution to combat the brutality of chattel slavery and the total erasure of their African identity and humanity.

This became war on the Spiritual level so Africans called on their divine spirits and their ancestors for help and guide them through the difficult times ahead. In Akan folklore there is a devine spirit named Anansi meaning spider. Today Anansi would be seen as a super hero with three super powers Wisdom, Shape-shifting and Trickery. The folklore exist among the Akan people to embody wisdom, survival and strength through intellect over brut force.

Spirituality and the memories of their homeland were all they possessed. This was the only inheritance that they could pass on to their future generations. They knew that for survival through all generations could only be possible in the Spiritual world as they believed that the spirit never dies it returned to the source.

Anansi offered all the qualities need for the Spiritual war against the enslavers. Only the strongest, wisest and the most versatile would be victorious in the Mental, Physical, and Spiritual dimensions. In African spirituality there is an intermediator and some cultures use a mask to represent the intermediator.

Banned from practicing African spirituality the slaves needed a Mask to represent Anansi. They devised an embodiment of the mask and called it Jab Jab. This is how the enslaved African celebrated Anansi as the son of the sky God and his mother the earth God. As Grenadian we inherited Jab Jab through culture and language coming from the French slave plantation to us today.

What survived the Middle passage, Chattel slavery and Colonization is Jab Jab. We are still Playing Mas in Grenadian patois meaning we are not the Mask we a just playing it.

Jab Jab is a product of Chattel slavery which reduced the African to property and less than an animal in the mind of the enslavers. As slaves they were forced to surrender their bodies but they retained their minds, soul, emotions, memories of their mother land. In order to retain the essence of being they needed their spirituality.

Jab Jab became the living embodiment of the mask like Jesus embodied the christian god symbolically and mediate for Christians. Jab Jab represent Anansi the son of the sky god and his mother the god of earth.

The enslaved Africans could now celebrate because they created a festival to celebrate Anansi the Wise Shape Shifting Trickster. Carnival

Jab Jab is We Story

The Bedrock of a people is their culture and language transport a people culture from generation to generation. Jab Jab appeared in the Grenadian patois during the era of French slave plantations. Jab Jab is a product of Chattel slavery which reduced the African to property and less than an animal in the mind of the enslavers.

The Code Noir or the French Slave Code governed enslaved Africans in the French colonies and this determined all enslaved Africans must be baptised Roman Catholic. This meant that the Africans brought to Grenada by the French to work on their plantations were Roman Catholic.

The French elite being Roman Catholic celebrated a Roman festival called “Carna Vale” before they abstained from certain indulgences like eating meat and drinking alcohol for the period of Lent.

Early French plantation life was left up to the planter with little oversight from France. This allowed the enslaved Africans to negotiate privileges from the planters in exchange for willing labour.

What started as a Roman festival for Romans universally became the Temple of Anansi the wise, shape-shifting trickster. Jab Jab became the embodiment of the mask that represents the spirit Anansi.

Forced Christianity coupled with forced illiteracy in chattel slavery blocked all pathways to the African spirits by removing the intermediators. Christianity is mono theistic and Jesus is the intermediator for the Christian god. Grenadian play Mas because they could not bring or make their Masks so they were forced to play the Mask.

In Grenadian patois there are expressions like: We playing Mas or You playing man.

The Unbroken Spirit of Jab Jab

The spirit of the Jab Jab did not only survive the worse crime committed against humanity but it left the Grenadian plantation and traveled the world and is worship internationally. Grenada remains the temple mound and every J’ouvet morning we play Mas with a feast of indulgence to celebrate the devine spirit Anansi and our ancestors as a reminder of who we are as Grenadians.

Chattel slavery reduced the African to property and less than an animal in the mind of the enslavers. As a dispossessed people they were forced to find simple but creative ways to achieve their objectives. Survival became the only option because they knew that they would not survive plantation slavery but their descendents would one day see the end of the maafa.

As a dispossessed people only knowledge and memories survived the middle passage and plantation slavery. The science of Epigenetics teaches that memory is retained in our DNA and can be recalled through the generation as what is hidden from the wise and prudent. This is the Treasure chest that our ancestor used to hide and preserve their knowledge and memories waiting for the babes and sucklings to find. We are the generation that has been through great tribulation and found that Treasure chest. That is our inheritance left to us by the ones on whose shoulders we stand today.

What Jab Jab is presenting to the world today through culture is a Celebration of our Ancestors for teaching us the lesson of survival through resistance and resilience intellectually the African way. Intellect over brute force

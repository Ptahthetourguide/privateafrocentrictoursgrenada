Jab Jab: Living Archaeology

The conch shell sounds as latecomers arrive for the pouring of the old oil. Once everyone is blackened, they visit the coffin to release the intoxicating spirit — rum — and pour libation to invite the ancestors. As the first rays of sun appear, it is J’ouvert morning.

This is the moment Jab Jab lives for: to play Mas. The warm‑up begins with another drink of rum. Now come the important parts of every Jab Jab costume — the horns, chain, and red mouth. J’ouvert starts with the sun and a pulsating Jab Jab rhythm, leading into the call‑and‑response chant. The conch shell conducts the rhythm section of old kitchen utensils and anything that can hold the beat. That is when this Spice nation starts Playing Mas.

The sensory experience is overwhelming: the smell of oil, BBQ chicken and rum, the sound of the drum echoing through narrow streets, and the sight of bodies covered in old oil, moving as one. This is living archaeology — a ritual that connects Grenadians to their ancestors through rhythm, costume, and the Jab Jab spirit.

What Is Jab Jab

Jab Jab is the living embodiment of the mask which represent the Mediator between the physical and the spiritual world. This culture started on the slave plantations as a solution to the brutality and the total erasure of their humanity. The Roman festival “Carna Vale” that the French elite celebrated before they abstained from certain indulgences like eating meat and drinking alcohol for the period of Lent became Carnival.

Banned from practicing African spirituality, slaves devised a mask to represent Anansi. In Akan folklore, Anansi — the spider — embodied wisdom, survival, and strength through Intellect over brute force. His stories taught that intellect and resilience could overcome oppression. They called it Jab Jab. What survived the Middle Passage, chattel slavery, and colonization is Jab Jab. A system our ancestors developed as a ways to surrender their bodies while retaining their humanity. We are still “Playing Mas” — in Grenadian patois meaning we are not the mask, we are just playing it.

Jab Jab is a cultural expression Grenadians created as an antidote to slavery and colonization.

Jab Jab Is We Story

The bedrock of a people is their culture and language transports that culture. La Jabless or La Diabless is the female Devil who is always well-dressed but had cow hoofs for feet. In Grenadian folklore she tempts men and boys into the lonely places only then revealing her true self. Due to amnesia her victims could not say what happened to them. The first known record of the word Jab in Grenadian patois is La Jabless.

The practice of forced Christianity as stipulated in the Le Code Noir that all enslaved Africans must be baptized Roman Catholic. Literacy for slaves was banned and punishable not only to the enslaved but anyone found teaching them to read or write. Unable to practice African spirituality and now way to document their life story, they passed down their knowledge and culture in various ways orally was one method. Storytelling, songs, work chants, proverbs are some of the ways which they transmitted culture and knowledge to retained their identity.

Erasure of their identity meant that slaves could not be seen practicing their spirituality so they created way to continue celebrating their Divine spirits. In African spirituality the physical world must use and intermediator to communicate with the spiritual world. In most West and Central African countries a mask is used to represent the intermediator. In patois we say that: “We playing Mas” or “You playing man.” What that means is that they were not allowed to have a mask to celebrate so they embodying the mask instead of making one.

This adaptation was survival. By disguising African spirituality within Jab Jab Africans transformed the Roman festival of “Carna Vale” into the Temple of Anansi, Carnival. This is how our ancestors preserved their heritage under the watchful eyes of colonizers. Jab Jab became both resistance and resilience, a coded language of survival that Anansi the wise trickster taught our ancestors.

The Unbroken Spirit of Jab Jab

The spirit of Jab Jab survived slavery and now travels the world, worshipped internationally. Grenada remains the temple mound, and every J’ouvert morning we play Mas to celebrate Anansi and our ancestors.

Today Anansi would be seen as a super hero with 3 weapons Wisdom, Shape-shifting and Trickery. The folklore exist among the Akan people to embody wisdom, survival and strength through intellect over brut force. This is Anansi in the manifestation as Jab Jab.

Epegenetics teaches that memory is retained in DNA and recalled through generations. Our ancestors hid knowledge in a “treasure chest” for future generations. Today, Jab Jab presents to the world a celebration of survival through wisdom, resistance and resilience — intellect over brute force.

Grenadians in the diaspora carried Jab Jab to cities like London, New York, and Toronto, where Carnival celebrations echo the rhythms of the Spice Isle. Jab Jab is no longer confined to Grenada; it is a global symbol of African survival and creativity.

Jab Jab and Grenada Carnival

Jab Jab is inseparable from Grenada Carnival. J’ouvert morning is the climax, when thousands of Grenadians take to the streets in oil, mud, and paint. The rhythm of Jab Jab drives Soca music, inspiring generations of artists and performers.

Jab Jab is not just entertainment; it is a cultural archive. Each chant, drumbeat, and costume tells the story of survival, resistance, and joy. Jab Jab is the heartbeat of Carnival, reminding Grenadians of their African roots.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is Grenada Jab Jab?
A: Jab Jab is a cultural expression created by enslaved Africans on the francophone Caribbean islands as an antidote to slavery and colonization..

Q: When does Grenada Carnival take place?
A: Grenada Carnival, also known as Spicemas, is held every August and the parade of the Masqueraders bands is the second Monday and Tuesday in August. From J’ouvert morning to Wednesday it is Mas, Jab Jab, Soca music, vibrant parades and good vibes.

Q: How can visitors experience Jab Jab?
A: The best way is to join J’ouvert morning celebrations. Many Grenada Tours offer cultural packages that include Jab Jab experiences.

Q: Why is Jab Jab important to Grenadian identity?
A: Jab Jab connects Grenadians to their African heritage, reminding them of survival through slavery and colonization. It is both cultural expression and spiritual resistance.

Conclusion

Jab Jab is more than a Carnival tradition. It is Grenada’s living link to Africa, a cultural inheritance passed down through generations. From the plantations of French colonization to the streets of modern Carnival, Jab Jab embodies resilience, creativity, and survival. Anansi
